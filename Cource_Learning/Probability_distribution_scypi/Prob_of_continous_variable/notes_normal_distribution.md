### Universal Step-by-Step Approach for Normal Distribution Problems
1. Understand the Question
    Identify:
    => Mean (μ)
    => Standard deviation (σ)
    => What probability range is being asked (above, below, between)?
    => Sample size (if asked about number of people/things).


### Real-World Examples of Normal Distribution
1. Education (Exam Scores) 
    1. Exam marks of a large class often follow a normal distribution.
    2. Helps in grading (e.g., setting cutoffs, percentiles).
2. Manufacturing (Quality Control) 
    1. Size/weight of manufactured products (like bottle filling, screw length).
    2. Used in Six Sigma to detect defects.
3. Finance (Stock Returns)
    1. Daily returns of stocks approximate a normal distribution.
    2. Helps in risk modeling, portfolio management.
4. Medicine (Health Metrics)
    1. Blood pressure, cholesterol levels, human height/weight → normally distributed.
    2. Doctors can say “your BP is in the top 10% range”.
5. Marketing (Customer Behavior) 
    1. Time spent on a website, purchase amount, response time → often follow normal distribution.
    2. Helps in customer segmentation & targeting.
6. Machine Learning
    1. Many algorithms assume data is normally distributed (e.g., Linear Regression errors).
    2. Feature scaling, z-scores, and hypothesis testing all rely on normal distribution.


### How You’d Use Normal Distribution in a Project
1. Suppose you’re building a health analytics app:
    1. Collect blood pressure readings of users.
    2. Fit them to a normal distribution (mean, std_dev).
    3. Give insights like:
        1. “You are in the top 20% risk group.”
        2. “80% of healthy people fall between 110–130 mmHg.”
    4. Use this to recommend lifestyle changes or flag outliers.
2. Another example → In a sales prediction project:
    1. Daily sales follow a bell curve.
    2. Normal distribution helps you set thresholds:
        1. Below X → alert (low sales day).
        2. Above Y → anomaly (possible bulk order).
